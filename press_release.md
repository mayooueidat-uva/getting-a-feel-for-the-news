# Getting a Feel for the News: Sentiment Analysis and Article Reliability
## It is necessary to quickly detect a piece of news' relability, but gauging the emotions conveyed in the headline may not be enough  
## Problem statement 
As a denizen of the Internet, you are likely caught beneath a cascade of information - not all of it equal in reliability. Therefore, we must investigate whether we can predict the reliability of an article using a tool called sentiment analysis, which allows us to gauge the strengh of emotions in its headline. If strong correlations between headline sentiment and reliability are found, we could quickly find potentially misinformative articles. 
## Solution description 
We have assembled a pipeline to serve as a prototype of an article reliability detection mechanism. If you are to feed a news article headline into the pipeline, our hope is that, with sentiment analysis, you can predict its reliability. Certain databases gauging source and article bias and reliability exist, where teams of experts have assigned scores to different news outlets in accordance with their set of standards. Using data from Ad Fontes Media, we have acquired reliability scores of various news sources, such as CNN and Fox; and using the VADER sentiment analyser (generally designed for social media posts), we have asssessed the level of emotion conveyed in article headlines. The algorithm we built did not effectively detect patterns between emotion strength and source reliability; in fact, emotion and reliability did not seem strongly correlated in the first place.
